SEQUENCE 01–06

The build order recruiters actually respond to

Follow it in order. Skipping straight to "make it pretty" is the most common reason portfolios go unread.

01

Pick one stack and go deep

A portfolio with one stack done well beats five stacks done shallowly. Choose MERN, Python, or another path — then commit long enough to build something non-trivial in it.

02

Build proof, not tutorials

Two or three projects that solve a real, specific problem will outperform ten clones of a to-do app. Pick problems from your own life, campus, or community — they're easier to explain with conviction.

03

Write case studies, not captions

For each project, document the problem, the decisions you made, the trade-offs, and what you'd do differently. This is what separates "I made an app" from "I can think like an engineer."

04

Ship it somewhere clickable

A live link beats a local screenshot every time. Deploy your projects, keep them running, and make sure a recruiter can click through without cloning a repo.

05

Clean up what's public

Pin your best repos, write real README files with setup steps and screenshots, and archive or hide the practice code that doesn't represent your current skill level.

06

Tailor it to the role you want

A generic portfolio speaks to no one in particular. Before you apply, adjust the order and framing of your projects to match the job description in front of you.
